## Project description draft

Tidal Loom is a small water garden that you mend by hand. A spring feeds a collection of round islands. Turn their channels until the water reaches every garden without spilling at an open edge. Flowers grow as each route comes together.

The board is designed for a seated session: one compact play space, no locomotion, no clock and no need to pair a controller. Pinch a channel, twist it, and release. A short pinch turns it one step. Undo, Reset, Next and Exit sit on the board. Mouse play and labeled buttons provide an accessible way to try the same puzzle on a screen.

The original prototype uses the Immersive Web SDK for hand input and grabbing, and a separate, deterministic channel model for the puzzle. Its geometry, flowers and optional chimes are generated in the application. It needs no remote model or paid service. Progress stays in browser storage.

The next development work is device testing, clearer hand onboarding and more varied islands. Current hand-interaction evidence comes from IWER; physical headset performance is not yet verified. Codex assisted with implementation, testing and written materials.
